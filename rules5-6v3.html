Rules 5-6

Rule 5

Error Prevention

Good error messages are important, but the best designs carefully prevent problems from occuring in the first place. Either eliminate error-prone conditions, or check for them and present users with a conrmation option before they commit to the action.


A good design should either stop users from making mistakes in the first place or provide a clear warning before they do something risky. Error messages should be expressed in plain language (no codes), precisely indicate the problem, and constructively suggest a solution.

Rule 6

Recognition Rather Than Recall

Minimize the user's memory load by making elements, actions, and options visible. The user should not have to remember information from one part of the interface to another. Information required to use the design should be visible or easily retrievable when needed.


Make things visible: Don't make users rely on their memory. Buttons, instructions, and options should be clearly visible and easy to find.
